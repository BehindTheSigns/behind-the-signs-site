Channel letters are one of the best jobs a shop can land. Good margin, repeat customers, and a sign that makes your work visible on the street for years. They're also where a lot of shops get hurt, and almost always for the same reason: something about the site wasn't known until install day.

A proper survey takes an hour. A missed detail costs a return trip, a re-fab, or a lost customer. Here's what to check before the quote goes out.

## The wall

- **What is it made of?** Brick, block, EIFS, metal panel, stucco over what? Each one changes your fasteners, your blocking, and sometimes whether a raceway is required.
- **Is there a parapet or a canopy?** Note where the letters will actually sit and what's behind that surface.
- **Photos, straight on and from both angles.** Take more than you think you need. Include a tape measure or a known reference in at least one shot.
- **Measure the sign band.** Height, width, and the distance from grade to the bottom of the band. Don't trust the landlord's drawing.

## Power

- **Where is the nearest circuit?** Find the electrical panel, confirm there's a dedicated circuit or room for one, and note the run distance to the sign location.
- **Who is providing the primary?** Get it in writing. "The electrician will handle it" is not a plan until you know which electrician and when.
- **Is there existing wiring from an old sign?** Check its condition. Reusing bad wire to save a customer money is a warranty call waiting to happen.
- **Transformer location.** Inside the raceway, behind the wall, or remote? Confirm access for service.

## Access

- **Lift or ladder?** Measure the ground clearance and note the surface. Grass, a sloped lot, or a drive-through lane changes the equipment and the day you can install.
- **Overhead obstructions.** Power lines, awnings, tree branches, neighboring signs.
- **Business hours.** Can you install during the day, or does the customer need it done before they open?

## Rules

- **Landlord sign criteria.** Many plazas have a document that dictates letter height, illumination type, raceway color, and mounting. Get it before you design anything.
- **Municipal code.** Maximum square footage, illumination limits, setback, and whether a permit requires stamped drawings.
- **Permit timeline.** Ask the city how long approvals take right now. Put that number in your schedule and tell the customer.

## The existing sign

- **Is there one?** Who removes it, who patches the wall, and who paints the patch? Put each of those in the quote or explicitly out of it.
- **Old mounting holes.** Note their pattern. Sometimes you can reuse them, and sometimes they tell you the wall has been patched before.

## Write it down

A survey that lives in someone's head is not a survey. Use a form, take the photos, and attach both to the job. When the installer opens the folder on the day, everything they need should already be there.

The best channel letter shops aren't the ones with the fanciest benders. They're the ones that never get surprised on the ladder.
